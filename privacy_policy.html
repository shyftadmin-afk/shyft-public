Privacy Policy

Effective from 1 November 2026 · Applies to the Shyft iOS app and its widget

Shyft offers short guided sessions, called “shyfts”, that help you change how you feel. This page explains what the app collects, why, and who handles it.

In short

  • We do not sell your data, show ads or track you across other apps.
  • When you create a shyft, our AI providers receive what you write or say, plus a short profile from your past sessions.
  • Usage analytics is on unless you turn it off in Settings → Privacy → Share usage analytics. It records how you use the app and the topics you pick, health-related ones too, linked to your account once you sign in.
  • Crash reports go to our diagnostics provider.
  • You can delete your account at any time in Settings → Account.
  • Questions: shyftadmin@gmail.com.

1. Who we are

Robert Camping (“we”) is the data controller. Contact us at shyftadmin@gmail.com.

Shyft is available in the UK, the US, Canada and Australia. We are based in the UK and follow UK data protection law wherever you live. Outside the UK, you also keep the rights your own law gives you.

Shyft needs an Apple or Google sign-in, so your library can follow you to another device. You can open a shared shyft without signing in.

Free shyfts. Every account can create three personalised shyfts free, three in all. If you delete an account after using any of them, we keep a one-way hash of your Apple or Google account identifier and the number used. So signing in again does not give you three new ones. Permissions.

  • Calendar: Shyft does not read it. With your permission, it can add a shyft you schedule.
  • Microphone and speech recognition: used when you record a shyft or say what you need. Apple turns speech into text, sometimes on its servers. We then use that text like anything you type.
  • Motion: read on your phone to time suggestions. The readings are not sent to us.

3. Usage analytics

We use PostHog to record how you use the app, including:

  • when the app is installed, updated, opened and closed;
  • the introduction screens, sign-in, the tabs you open and the home rows you see;
  • which shyfts, tools, flows and packs you start, finish, save, rate or schedule, from where, and how long you listen;
  • time in the swipe feed, the flows you build, and how many schedules and created shyfts you have;
  • in Create: the mode, voice and length of what you wrote, never the words, and whether it worked;
  • the notifications you allow and open;
  • links that open the app, without the private part of share links;
  • problems such as audio that would not play.

PostHog also receives the topics you pick, including the health-related ones: grief, anxiety, low mood, drinking, urges and cravings, gambling, food and eating, ADHD and OCD.

For the shyfts we list under those topics, and for those in adult-only packs, PostHog records that you played, saved, scheduled or rated one, not which one. Other shyfts and tools are recorded by name, with their topic. That includes the packs we suggest for those topics, such as Anxiety, Low Mood and Bereavement, and tools named after a therapy, such as EMDR.

It also receives where you heard about Shyft. The only text you type that it receives is a creator name, if you give one.

Each record carries:

  • a random ID for this installation;
  • your account ID, once you sign in (what you did before signing in is then linked to it);
  • a session ID;
  • your device model and screen size, iOS and app version, and whether it is a test build;
  • your language, time zone and region setting, and whether you use Wi-Fi or mobile data.

PostHog receives your IP address with each connection. We have set it not to work out your location. While analytics is on, our server also tells PostHog whether creating a shyft worked. PostHog never receives your name, email address, what you write or say in Create, or your recordings.

We also use PostHog to change the introduction, the home screen and how we ask about notifications without an app update, sometimes showing two versions to compare them.

To turn it off, go to Settings → Privacy → Share usage analytics. This stops new data but does not delete what was already sent (section 7).

4. Who handles your data

These providers process data for us under written agreements. Each may use it only to provide its service to us, and must protect it as this policy does:

  • Supabase: your account, synced library, free-shyft count and the hash.
  • Cloudflare: our server, usage counters, share links, and the audio and artwork we make for you.
  • Anthropic: writes your shyft, or finds a ready-made one, from what you wrote or said and your profile.
  • OpenAI: draws the artwork, normally from a short scene description. If that step fails, it receives the shyft’s title instead.
  • ElevenLabs: turns the script into speech.
  • Sentry: crash and performance reports.
  • PostHog: usage analytics, hosted in the EU (Frankfurt). PostHog is a US company and its staff may access data from outside the UK and EU.

Some of these providers are in the US. For those transfers we rely on the UK Addendum to the EU standard contractual clauses. Where a provider takes part, we also rely on the UK Extension to the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. Ask us for a copy of these safeguards.

Apple and Google handle their own sign-in under their own privacy policies. If iCloud is on, Apple keeps your streaks and audio settings, and your recordings and the audio and artwork of shyfts you created sit in your own iCloud Drive. Apple also handles speech recognition. The web player for shared shyfts loads fonts from Google Fonts and Fontshare, which see your IP address.

Feedback you write in the app is stored without your account ID. Feedback after a session also names the shyft. The feed is not private, so please leave out personal details. We cannot find your feedback unless you quote it. Your tap on “Did this help?” goes to PostHog as usage analytics. What you write there does not.

5. Our legal bases

  • Contract: running your account, creating the shyfts you ask for (including sending your words to our AI providers), and the links you share.
  • Legitimate interests: crash and performance reports, keeping the app secure, preventing abuse of free shyfts (including the hash), and usage analytics.
  • Consent: calendar, microphone, speech recognition and notifications. You can withdraw it in iOS Settings.
  • Explicit consent: we ask before what you write or say about your health or state of mind goes to our AI providers.

You need an account to use Shyft. Everything else is optional: saying no to a permission or to AI consent turns off only that feature. Withdrawing consent does not affect what was done before.

UK law lets us use the analytics IDs on your phone for statistics and testing improvements without asking first. We must tell you, and this page does. You must be able to object for free, which you can with the switch in section 3.

Shyft’s automatic suggestions only choose which audio to offer and when. They have no legal or similarly significant effect.

6. How long we keep it

  • Account and library: until you delete your account.
  • Audio and artwork we make for you: until you send us the links and ask us to remove them. Nothing deletes them automatically. They sit at random, unlisted links that are not tied to your account.
  • Share links: library shyfts expire after 24 hours. Shyfts you created stay live until you revoke them. Older gift links, with the name and message, expire after 30 days, but the audio behind them stays as above.
  • Usage counters: up to 35 days.
  • The free-shyft hash: 24 months after you delete your account.
  • Crash reports: up to about 90 days.
  • Usage analytics: one year in PostHog. The IDs on your phone stay until you delete the app.
  • Anonymous feedback: kept indefinitely.

We may keep data longer where the law requires it or to deal with a legal claim.

7. Deleting your data

Go to Settings → Account → Delete account. This permanently removes your profile, synced library, free-shyft count and generation history from our servers. What is on your phone goes when you use Settings → Privacy → Reset Shyft or delete the app. What is in your iCloud stays until you delete it.

Some things stay after you delete your account:

  • the audio and artwork we made for you. Send us the links and we will delete them;
  • share links. Revoke them first in Settings → Privacy → Shared by You, which also deletes the copy of the audio the link plays. It works only on the phone you sent them from, and not after reinstalling the app. Otherwise, email us the links;
  • the free-shyft hash, for 24 months;
  • crash reports and anonymous feedback, as in section 6;
  • usage analytics. We delete it if you ask. Please ask before deleting your account, because afterwards we cannot tell which data was yours. We cannot find anything recorded after you signed out.

8. Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your data. You can also ask us to correct, delete or transfer it, or to restrict how we use it. Where we rely on your consent, you can withdraw it.

Your right to object. Where we rely on legitimate interests (crash reports, abuse prevention and usage analytics), you can object at any time. The Share usage analytics switch stops analytics at once. For the rest, email us.

Write to shyftadmin@gmail.com. Requests are usually free. We reply without undue delay and within one month of confirming who you are. If a request is complex, we may take up to two more months, and we will tell you why within the first month.

If you are unhappy, tell us. We will acknowledge your complaint within 30 days and tell you the outcome. You can also complain to the UK Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk or to the regulator where you live.

9. Children, security and changes

Children. Shyft is not for children under 13. If you think a child has given us data, contact us and we will delete it.

Security. Data travels encrypted, and access rules are designed so that each account can read only its own data. The anonymous feedback feed is the exception. Audio and artwork we make, and shyfts you share, sit at public links with no password, so people without the app can open them. Treat a shared link as public. No system is perfectly secure, so please think before you share details about other people.

Not medical care. Shyft supports everyday wellbeing. It is not a medical service and not for emergencies. If you need help now, see our support page.

Changes. When we update this policy, we post the new version here with a new date. If the law requires it, we will tell you or ask for your consent again.
